Statistics for Machine Learning · Reference

Glossary

The canonical meaning & notation for every recurring term in this course. Once a symbol is defined here, every lesson uses it the same way. Keep this tab open.

Core objects
Random variableX
A function assigning a number to each outcome of a random process. Discrete (countable outcomes) or continuous (a range). Capital letters (X) denote the variable; lowercase (x) a specific value it takes.
ProbabilityP(X = x)
How often outcome x occurs in the long run. For discrete X, the PMF; for continuous X, the PDF f(x) (density — area under it gives probability).
Expectation / expected valueE[X], μ
The probability-weighted average of a random variable — its long-run mean. See Lesson 1.
VarianceVar(X), σ²
The expected squared distance from the mean: E[(X − μ)²]. Spread of a random variable. Standard deviation σ = √Var(X) is in the variable's own units.
Estimation
Estimator / estimateμ̂, σ̂
A quantity computed from data to approximate a true (usually unknown) parameter. The "hat" marks an estimate. The sample mean x̄ is an estimate of E[X].
Parameterθ
A fixed but usually unknown number describing a distribution or model (e.g. a Normal's μ and σ).
Sample vs. population
Population: the full process/distribution (true, often unseen). Sample: the finite data you actually observe and compute from.
ML-facing
Conditional probabilityP(y | x)
Probability of y given that x is known. Most supervised ML models estimate P(y | x) or its argmax.
argmaxargmaxₓ f(x)
The input x that makes f largest (vs. max, which is the value). A classifier predicts argmax_y P(y | x).
Bias–variance
Decomposition of a model's expected error into systematic error (bias) and sensitivity to the training sample (variance). A future lesson.
