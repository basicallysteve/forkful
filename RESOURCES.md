# Resources

Trusted resources that ground the lessons. Lessons cite these directly. Rated
for **trust** (authority/quality) and tagged by role.

Legend: ⭐ = primary recommendation for its topic.

## Foundational courses & textbooks (knowledge)

- ⭐ **MIT 6.041 — Introduction to Probability** (Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis), MIT
  OpenCourseWare. The gold-standard applied-probability course; free video +
  lecture notes. Best for random variables, expectation, distributions,
  estimation, limit theorems.
  https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/
  Trust: very high (MIT, canonical text).

- ⭐ **Mathematics for Machine Learning** (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong), free PDF.
  Ch. 6 "Probability & Distributions" is exactly the ML-facing subset of stats.
  https://mml-book.github.io/
  Trust: very high (Cambridge University Press; ML-specific).

- **Think Stats, 2nd ed.** (Allen B. Downey), free online. Statistics taught
  entirely in Python — matches the mission's "applied + Python" framing.
  https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-stats-2e/
  Trust: high (widely used; Python-native).

- **Seeing Theory** (Daniel Kunin et al., Brown University). Beautiful
  interactive visual intro to probability & statistics — great for intuition
  before the math.
  https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/
  Trust: high (Brown; peer-praised).

## Video (intuition first)

- ⭐ **StatQuest with Josh Starmer** (YouTube / statquest.org). Short, intuitive,
  ML-oriented explanations. Excellent for expected value, distributions,
  p-values, likelihood, bias–variance.
  https://statquest.org/  |  Expected values: https://statquest.org/expected-values-clearly-explained/
  Trust: high (beloved in the ML community).

- **3Blue1Brown — Probability / "But what is..." series** (YouTube). Deep visual
  intuition for distributions, Bayes, central limit theorem.
  https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown
  Trust: high.

## Reference (look-up, not learning)

- **SciPy stats documentation** — the API for every distribution used in lessons.
  https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/stats.html
  Trust: very high (official).

- **StatLect** (Marco Taboga) — clean, rigorous online probability reference.
  https://www.statlect.com/
  Trust: high.

## Community (wisdom — test skills with real people)

- **r/learnmachinelearning** and **r/statistics** — active, beginner-friendly Q&A.
- **Cross Validated** (stats.stackexchange.com) — the definitive statistics Q&A
  site; high signal, expert answers.
- University course forums / study groups — highest value once the ML course starts.
