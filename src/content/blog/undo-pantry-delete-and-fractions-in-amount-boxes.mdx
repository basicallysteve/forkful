Two things came out of the kitchen recently. One is small and makes the pantry less nerve-wracking. The other is a bigger idea I've been working toward, and the first pieces of it are in. Both come from the same place: I want the experience to be the best it can be for our chefs.

## Fresh out of the oven: Undo on pantry delete

Deleting something from your pantry used to be final the moment you clicked. If you hit the wrong row, you had to add the item back and re-enter its quantity and expiry.

Now when you delete a pantry item, it leaves the list straight away and a toast appears: "Deleted Chicken Breast", with an Undo button. Click Undo and the item comes back in the same place in the list. If you don't, the delete goes through after a few seconds. Selecting several rows and choosing Delete gives you one toast for the whole batch, and one Undo brings them all back. If a delete fails on the server, the rows come back and you get the usual error message.

A pantry is something you tidy in a hurry, usually with a bag in one hand. Being able to take a delete back means you can clear things out quickly without checking each click twice.

## On the stove: serving definitions

Grams and millilitres are exact and precise. They're also not how anyone cooks. We live in a human world, with human servings.

My protein powder is 1 scoop = 30 g. I don't have time to sit over a food scale for every meal, but tracking and understanding my nutrition is important to me. Serving definitions are meant to bridge that gap: a food can say what one scoop weighs, so the app can turn a scoop into grams and keep the estimate accurate. The engine that does that conversion is built. I'm really excited about setting a food's scoop on screen, the next piece. Once it's in, a scoop can just be a scoop.

What has landed so far:

- **More count units.** Scoop, stick, tablet, pouch, punnet, bar and packet join the existing ones. You'll find them in the unit picker when you add or edit a food.
- **Fractions in amount boxes.** In the food log, the recipe ingredient rows and the pantry form, you can type "1 1/2" or "½" in an amount box and it understands you. It tidies the display to 1½ when you leave the field. The boxes are also properly labelled for screen readers.
- **The conversion engine underneath.** If a unit has a known weight, a count like "2 scoops" converts to grams. If it only has a volume, it goes to millilitres. When both are known, grams win. If the app can't work it out, it says so rather than guessing.
- **Pantry deduction and pantry gap use it.** Say a slice is 28 g and you have 100 g in the pantry. Using 2 slices now suggests taking off 56 g and leaves you with 44 g. Where the app can't convert, you still enter the amount yourself.
